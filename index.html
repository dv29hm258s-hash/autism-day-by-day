If your child has autism

You’re a member of a club you never asked to join. There’s no orientation, no instruction manual, and apparently, you’re supposed to figure out the rules as you go.

And you’re about to learn something cruel:

The most important information usually arrives in the wrong order.

You learn about services your child was entitled to after years of struggling. You learn how the IEP process really works after a service is denied. You learn about the adulthood cliff when school ends and the services disappear.

Somebody should tell families what is coming before they are standing in the middle of it.
