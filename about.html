About

I’m Donna Ross-Jones.

If your child has autism, you’re a member of a club you never asked to join. There’s no orientation, no instruction manual, and apparently, you’re supposed to figure out the rules as you go.

And you’re about to learn something cruel:

The most important information usually arrives in the wrong order.

You learn about services your child was entitled to after years of struggling, paying out of pocket, or doing without.

You learn how the IEP process really works after a service is denied or taken away, and only then discover you had the right to fight for it.

You learn about wandering after those terrifying minutes searching for your child, praying someone finds them before a car does.

You learn about seizures after watching your child collapse, shaking, wondering if you’re watching them die.

You learn about the adulthood cliff when school ends and the services your child depended on disappear. Suddenly, there’s nowhere to go, and you were supposed to have planned for this years ago.

How are you supposed to ask for help you don’t even know exists?

Everybody is incredibly helpful about ten minutes too late.

I know. Because that’s how I learned almost everything.

I’m Donna Ross-Jones. My son Nick is 28 and has profound autism. For nearly three decades, we’ve lived through therapies, school battles, seizures, puberty, safety scares, sibling relationships, and the transition into adulthood—including the question every parent whose child may need lifelong support eventually faces: What happens when I’m gone?

I started advocating for families 26 years ago because I needed the map I never got. These guides are that map, written in the order I wish someone had handed it to me.

It’s real. It’s unedited. It’s 24/7.
